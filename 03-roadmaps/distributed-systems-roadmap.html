Distributed Systems Roadmap

Distributed Systems Roadmap

New

Jun 16, 20263 min read

Distributed systems are the one corner of engineering where the textbook answer is almost always “it depends” — and the part that depends is usually a coordination cost or a failure mode that didn’t exist in the single-machine world. This roadmap reads the published notes in the order that lets each one stand on the last, instead of dropping you into Paxos without ever explaining why “now” is the hardest word in the language.

The first leg is what changes when there is no shared clock and no shared memory. The middle leg is what coordination buys you and what it costs. The last leg is what production looks like once you’ve stopped pretending the network is reliable.

Read in order

Foundations — time, identity, failure

  1. Distributed Systems Primitives — Network partitions, partial failure, why “exactly once” is a marketing term. The vocabulary every later note assumes.
  2. Logical Clocks — Lamport, vector, hybrid logical clocks. The moment you accept that wall-clock time can’t order events across machines is the moment the rest of the field stops feeling arbitrary.
  3. CAP and PACELC — The trade-off everyone quotes and almost no one quotes correctly. PACELC is the version that survives contact with real systems because it accounts for the latency cost during normal operation, not just the partition.

Coordination — consensus, locks, transactions

  1. Consensus Algorithm — Paxos, Raft, Multi-Paxos. The minimum machinery to agree on a single value when machines can crash and recover.
  2. Distributed Lock — Why a Redis SETNX is not a lock, fencing tokens, and when consensus is overkill versus when it’s table stakes.
  3. MVCC — How modern databases give you snapshot isolation without the lock storms of the alternatives. The bridge note between distributed-systems theory and database internals.
  4. Distributed Transactions — Two-phase commit, Sagas, the operational cost of each. The honest answer is usually “don’t” — this note explains when “don’t” stops being an option.

Partitioning, replication, membership

  1. Consistent Hashing — The partitioning primitive that every NoSQL system inherits. Virtual nodes, jump hash, why naïve hash(key) % N is a re-shuffle bomb on every resize.
  2. Gossip Protocol — Anti-entropy, SWIM, how Cassandra and Consul learn cluster membership without a coordinator. Epidemic spread is the only thing that scales O(log N) hops for free.

Operational reality

  1. Microservices — What you actually buy by paying the distributed-systems tax, and what you pay for buying it accidentally. The note to read last because every chapter before it is what makes the trade-offs concrete.

Where to go next

  • System design — the APIs & Networking series for the layer above, and the Production Systems Deep Dives for what running these primitives in real systems looks like (Kafka, Spanner, DynamoDB).
  • Architecture — the Architecture & Patterns index covers BFF, CQRS, fan-out, and the patterns you reach for once you’ve decided to actually go distributed.
  • AI Systems — see the AI Systems Roadmap for the same dependency-ordered treatment of embeddings, RAG, and model serving.

This roadmap is a living document. As more notes get published from the vault, the dependency graph grows — the order above is what reads best with what exists today.
